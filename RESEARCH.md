# Highway Roop corporate website — design and content rationale

## Positioning

The redesign moves Highway Roop from a holding-page and product-catalogue model to a corporate engineering platform. The intended audience is global OEMs, Tier-1 customers, partners, prospective employees and institutional stakeholders.

The homepage narrative is: scale and confidence → corporate identity → three complementary businesses → integrated capabilities → markets and systems → global reach → future mobility → responsible growth → commercial contact.

## Findings from the current ecosystem

- The parent website communicates useful scale figures and leadership, but does not yet express the depth of the combined manufacturing platform.
- Highway Industries has substantial content for manufacturing, engineering, products, sustainability and EV readiness, but its visual hierarchy is catalogue-led.
- Highway Roop Precision Technologies has strong steering, driveline, facilities and customer proof, but the content architecture is dense and brand experience dated.
- Chamundi adds a strategically important aluminium casting story: HPDC/GDC, tooling, machining, powder coating, four facilities and EV relevance.
- The three companies should be shown as complementary specialists within one operating system, not as unrelated outbound links.

## Benchmark takeaways

- Bharat Forge uses cinematic industrial imagery, large statements, leadership/legacy storytelling, facility-led navigation and deep corporate information.
- Vedanta uses a clear corporate narrative, business portfolio architecture, global footprint, sustainability/social impact and current communications.
- For Highway Roop, the strongest combination is Bharat Forge’s engineering authority with Vedanta’s portfolio clarity—without copying either layout.

## Recommended information architecture

1. Home
2. About: overview, story, values, leadership, milestones
3. Businesses: group overview plus profiles for Highway Roop Industries, Highway Roop Precision Technologies and Chamundi Die Cast
4. Products & Solutions: organised by vehicle system/application
5. Capabilities: R&D, tooling, forging, casting, machining, stamping, heat treatment, coating, assembly, validation
6. Markets: passenger, commercial, two-wheeler, off-highway, agriculture, industrial and EV
7. Global Presence: plants, warehouses and markets
8. Innovation: ICE/EV, lightweighting, automation, new product development
9. Quality: systems, testing, metrology and certification library
10. Sustainability: environment, people/community and governance
11. Careers
12. Media & Downloads
13. Contact

## Visual system

- Deep navy and steel neutrals establish engineering credibility; cyan creates continuity with the current Highway identity; red is retained as a restrained Roop cue.
- Manrope carries large corporate headlines; DM Sans handles readable interface and body copy.
- Square geometry, fine rules, industrial grids and disciplined motion keep the aesthetic technical and premium.
- One original cinematic factory image leads the story. Remaining sections rely on typography, data, diagrams and structured surfaces to avoid generic stock-photo overload.

## Reference-led redesign — final direction

The second design pass was rebuilt after reviewing DENSO, Magna, Aptiv and Hyundai Mobis in Chrome.

- DENSO: full-bleed editorial motion, quiet navigation and human-scale storytelling rather than a product catalogue.
- Magna: confident dark/white contrast, direct corporate language and strong proof through operating scale.
- Aptiv: disciplined typographic hierarchy, sharp red accents, motion-led automotive context and highly structured content.
- Hyundai Mobis: minimal full-screen pacing, few interface elements and distinct narrative chapters.

The final Highway Roop design combines these principles without reproducing any reference layout. It removes decorative dashboards and generic feature cards, uses Josefin Sans throughout, applies only the approved `#046684` and `#fd0000` accents, and introduces the supplied Highway Roop logo in both the header and footer.

The hero uses licensed automotive-factory footage from Pexels (Engin Altundağ, asset 6450803) as preview media. Before production launch, it should be replaced with Highway Roop-owned plant, machining, forging and team footage for full brand authenticity.
